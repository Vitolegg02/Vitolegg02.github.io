My research interests lie primarily in political economy 
and development economics, particularly studying the behavior of politicians in non-democratic regimes.

## Work in Progress

### Reelection Incentives and Local Public Performance. With Evidence from a Municipal Reform in Mexico 

This paper studies how electoral career concerns affect local public performance. I develop a moral hazard model and compare the combination of explicit and im plicit incentives with the case of bonus payments only. I find that career concerns are more effective in smaller communities with tighter competition, while bonus payments become more relevant otherwise. I test these predictions using a reform introducing reelection for municipal presidents in Mexico, and exploiting the stag gered implementation across states. A difference-in-differences estimation shows that reelection increases municipal nighttime luminosity by about 0.25 log points. The effect is robust across specifications, stronger in smaller municipalities, and weaker for incumbents who won by large margins.

### Trade Relationships and Voting at the UN over the Russian-Ukrainian Conflict

The Russian-Ukrainian conflict that started in February 2022 was quite a shock to the whole European and World community. In this Thesis Project I focus on how countries vote in UN General Assembly resolutions related to this conflict and how their votes are affected by international trade. I also look at the relation between sanctions and said trade connections. I find that a doubling in trade with the Russian Federation decreases the probability of a country to vote in favor of an anti-Russian resolution by 3.5 percentage points. Trade per Capita appears to be more important with a coefficient of -6.5 percentage points for Russia and 9 percentage points for Ukraine. I do also find that Imports of Russian Cereals in particular have a strong effect, unlike other commodities. Finally, I conduct an instrumental regression using the accident in the Suez canal as an instrument for trade. I do not find any significant coefficients in this specification, though that might be attributable to the locality of the variability in trade provided by the instrument.

### The Cost of Uneducated Children: Externalities, Social Norms, and the Quantity-Quality Tradeof 

My project develops a theoretical model of the quantity-quality tradeoff in fer tility, incorporating negative externalities where a household’s cost of education increases with the number of non-educating neighbors. I show that this can lead to multiple equilibria and that policies reducing the direct cost of children can counter intuitively decrease total human capital by triggering a shift to a worse equilibrium or amplifying negative spillovers. These results, which hold for both discrete and continuous populations, call for caution in designing fertility-inducing policies.

### An OLG Model of Conflict and Production A Study of Conflict, Corruption, and Foreign Aid

I build a model simulating the interaction between competing groups in a single country, where each group can spend time on peaceful labor and conflict. Similarly to other papers I show that conflict increases with potential gains and decreases with opportunity costs in wages. By endogenizing both the benefit and wages with a CES production function, I find that when capital and labor are complements, there is a large possibility of poverty traps to arise. I also provide a brief discussion on why foreign aid should be applied very carefully in settings with potential for conflict.

### Dutch Disease or Agglomeration? The Case of Military Expenditures

This paper studies how counties with greater preexisting military exposure re spond to changes in federal defense procurement. I build a Bartik-style measure that combines counties’ baseline military employment shares with national defense spending and estimate its effects using a staggered difference-in-differences design with continuous treatment. Defense expansions raise employment, income, income per capita, and rents in more exposed counties, but population, military employ ment, and Republican presidential vote share all decline. Sectoral estimates show little response in manufacturing and gains in agriculture and science and technol ogy services. Overall, defense spending appears to generate local economic benefits through channels beyond a simple expansion of military activity.

### Do Religious Leaders Matter? An Empirical Study on Transitions and Votings in Russia

This paper investigates whether natural leadership transitions in the Russian Or thodox Church influence regional vote shares for Vladimir Putin. Using a manually collected dataset on bishop deaths and retirements between 2000 and 2025, I employ a staggered difference-in-differences regression design. I find no significant evidence that bishop transitions affect electoral outcomes, a result that holds across multiple robustness checks. While the creation of new dioceses is correlated with higher vote shares, this relationship is likely endogenous. The findings suggest that the political influence of religious leaders in modern Russia may be quite limited. However, the zero results might also be caused by noisy data or heterogeneous effects.

### Churches, Religion and Development. A Broad Empirical Study from Russia

This project is a broad study of Religion and Development in Russia. I create an instrument for the number of churches in the regions of Russia using exogenous variation in the deportation of clerus workers in the Soviet repressions of 1939. I find that churches apparently do not affect most aspects of development at the regional level, except perhaps for some specific criminal offenses. Two clear limitations of these results are the rather noisy regional-level data and the locality of the treatment effects of my instrument. Since this is pioneering work for Russia, I do not examine any mechanisms in detail, but rather broadly describe the whole picture in general.
